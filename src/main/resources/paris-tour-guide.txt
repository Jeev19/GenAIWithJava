-----------------------------------------------------
1. PARIS AT A GLANCE
-----------------------------------------------------
- Currency: Euro (EUR). Cards accepted almost everywhere;
  carry a little cash for markets and small cafes.
- Language: French. English is widely understood in
  tourist areas, but a polite "Bonjour" first goes a long way.
- Power: Type C/E plugs, 230V. Indian chargers with the
  two round pins usually fit Type C sockets directly.
- Layout: The city is split into 20 "arrondissements"
  (districts) spiralling out clockwise from the centre.
  The Seine divides it into the Right Bank (Rive Droite,
  north) and Left Bank (Rive Gauche, south).
- Best time to visit: April to June and September to
  October. July and August are hot and crowded; winter
  is cold and grey but quieter, with festive lights in
  December.
- Visa (for Indian passport holders): a Schengen visa is
  required. Apply well ahead through the French visa
  centre (VFS Global) in your city.


-----------------------------------------------------
2. GETTING AROUND
-----------------------------------------------------
METRO / RER
- The Metro is the fastest way around. 16 lines,
  stations every few hundred metres.
- RER trains connect the centre to airports, Versailles
  and Disneyland.
- Buy tickets in the official "Bonjour RATP" or
  "IDF Mobilites" apps, or load a Navigo Easy card at a
  station. A Navigo weekly pass (Monday to Sunday) is good
  value if you're staying a full week.

FROM THE AIRPORTS
- Charles de Gaulle (CDG): RER B to Gare du Nord /
  Chatelet (about 35 to 45 min), or Roissybus to Opera.
- Orly (ORY): Metro line 14 now runs directly to the
  centre, or the Orlyval + RER B combination.
- Taxis from both airports charge fixed flat fares to
  central Paris (Right Bank / Left Bank rates). Use only
  the official taxi rank.

WALKING
- Paris is very walkable. Many central sights are 15 to 30
  minutes apart on foot, and walking is the best way to
  see the city.

BIKES
- Velib' is the city bike-share (regular and electric).
  Paris has added many protected bike lanes recently.

RIVER
- Batobus is a hop-on hop-off boat along the Seine with
  stops at major sights.


-----------------------------------------------------
3. THE MUST-SEE SIGHTS
-----------------------------------------------------
EIFFEL TOWER (7th)
- Book a timed ticket online. Summit tickets sell out.
- Walking up the stairs to the 2nd floor is cheaper and
  queues are often shorter.
- Best views OF the tower: Trocadero (across the river),
  Champ de Mars, and Pont de Bir-Hakeim.
- It sparkles for 5 minutes on the hour after dark.

THE LOUVRE (1st)
- Closed on Tuesdays. Book a timed entry slot.
- It's enormous. Pick 3 or 4 highlights rather than
  trying to see it all: Mona Lisa, Venus de Milo, Winged
  Victory of Samothrace, the Egyptian antiquities,
  Napoleon III Apartments.
- Enter via the Carrousel du Louvre or Porte des Lions
  entrances to avoid the main pyramid queue.

NOTRE-DAME CATHEDRAL (Ile de la Cite)
- Reopened in December 2024 after the 2019 fire. Entry
  to the cathedral is free; reserving a time slot online
  helps you skip the line.
- The towers can be climbed with a separate ticket.

SAINTE-CHAPELLE (Ile de la Cite)
- A 13th-century chapel with floor-to-ceiling stained
  glass. Go on a sunny day, ideally morning. Book ahead.

ARC DE TRIOMPHE & CHAMPS-ELYSEES (8th)
- Climb the Arc for one of the best panoramic views,
  looking straight down the Champs-Elysees.
- Use the underground passage to reach it; never try to
  cross the roundabout.

MONTMARTRE & SACRE-COEUR (18th)
- Hilltop basilica with sweeping city views. Free entry.
- Wander Place du Tertre (artists' square), Rue de
  l'Abreuvoir and the Montmartre vineyard.
- Watch for "friendship bracelet" scammers at the steps.

MUSEE D'ORSAY (7th)
- Impressionist masterpieces (Monet, Renoir, Van Gogh,
  Degas) in a former railway station. Closed Mondays.
- Don't miss the view through the giant clock window.

PALAIS GARNIER (9th)
- The opulent opera house. Self-guided tours available
  daily; the grand staircase is spectacular.

LE MARAIS (3rd/4th)
- Historic district with Place des Vosges, boutiques,
  galleries, and great falafel on Rue des Rosiers.


-----------------------------------------------------
4. NEIGHBOURHOODS WORTH WANDERING
-----------------------------------------------------
- Saint-Germain-des-Pres (6th): literary cafes, bookshops,
  Luxembourg Gardens nearby.
- Latin Quarter (5th): student area, Shakespeare and
  Company bookshop, the Pantheon, narrow medieval lanes.
- Canal Saint-Martin (10th): relaxed, local, great for
  an evening stroll and canal-side picnics.
- Ile Saint-Louis (4th): tiny, quiet island. Get ice
  cream at Berthillon.
- Belleville (20th): multicultural, street art, and a
  great view from Parc de Belleville.
- Batignolles (17th): village feel, markets, few tourists.


-----------------------------------------------------
5. MUSEUMS BEYOND THE LOUVRE
-----------------------------------------------------
- Musee de l'Orangerie: Monet's giant Water Lilies.
- Musee Rodin: The Thinker, in a lovely garden.
- Centre Pompidou: modern art. NOTE: closed for a
  multi-year renovation that began in 2025; check status.
- Musee Picasso (Marais).
- Musee Carnavalet: free, the history of Paris.
- Catacombs: underground ossuary. Book well ahead; tickets
  sell out. Not for the claustrophobic.
- Paris Museum Pass: covers 50+ museums and monuments
  (2, 4 or 6 days). Worth it if you'll visit 3+ paid
  sights a day. You still need to reserve time slots at
  some places.
- Many national museums are free on the first Sunday of
  certain months; expect crowds.


-----------------------------------------------------
6. DAY TRIPS
-----------------------------------------------------
- Palace of Versailles: 40 min by RER C. Closed Mondays.
  Go early, book a timed ticket, allow a full day for the
  palace, gardens and Marie Antoinette's estate.
- Giverny: Monet's house and garden (open roughly April
  to October). Train to Vernon, then shuttle/bus.
- Disneyland Paris: RER A to Marne-la-Vallee.
- Fontainebleau: grand chateau and forest, ~40 min by train.
- Mont-Saint-Michel: long day (or overnight); best by tour
  or train + bus.
- Reims / Epernay: Champagne houses, ~45 min by TGV.


-----------------------------------------------------
7. FOOD AND DRINK
-----------------------------------------------------
WHAT TO TRY
- Croissant, pain au chocolat (from a bakery, not a cafe)
- Baguette tradition, crepes and galettes (savoury buckwheat)
- Croque monsieur, steak frites, French onion soup
- Macarons, eclairs, mille-feuille, tarte Tatin
- Cheese: Comte, Brie de Meaux, Camembert, Roquefort
- Falafel in the Marais

VEGETARIAN TIP
- Paris is easier for vegetarians than it used to be, but
  traditional bistros are meat-heavy. Look for
  "vegetarien" on menus, crepe shops, falafel spots,
  and Indian restaurants around Passage Brady and
  La Chapelle (10th/18th, "Little India").

ETIQUETTE
- Say "Bonjour" when entering any shop or cafe.
- Service is included in the bill ("service compris");
  rounding up or leaving a euro or two is appreciated but
  not required.
- Ask for the bill: "L'addition, s'il vous plait." Waiters
  won't bring it until you ask.
- Tap water is free: ask for "une carafe d'eau".
- Lunch "formule" (set menu) is the best-value meal.
- Restaurants usually serve lunch ~12:00 to 14:30 and
  dinner from ~19:30.

MARKETS
- Marche des Enfants Rouges (Marais, oldest covered market)
- Marche d'Aligre (12th)
- Rue Cler (7th) market street


-----------------------------------------------------
8. SUGGESTED ITINERARIES
-----------------------------------------------------
3 DAYS (THE CLASSICS)

Day 1: Islands and Left Bank
- Morning: Notre-Dame, then Sainte-Chapelle
- Lunch: Latin Quarter
- Afternoon: Shakespeare and Company, Luxembourg Gardens,
  Saint-Germain cafes
- Evening: Seine river cruise at sunset

Day 2: Louvre to Eiffel Tower
- Morning: Louvre (pre-booked, opening time)
- Midday: Tuileries Garden, Place de la Concorde
- Afternoon: Musee d'Orsay or Champs-Elysees + Arc de
  Triomphe
- Evening: Eiffel Tower (book sunset slot), then watch it
  sparkle from Trocadero

Day 3: Montmartre and Marais
- Morning: Sacre-Coeur and Montmartre's lanes
- Afternoon: Palais Garnier, then Le Marais, Place des
  Vosges, falafel on Rue des Rosiers
- Evening: Canal Saint-Martin or a bistro dinner

5 DAYS
- Days 1 to 3 as above
- Day 4: Versailles (full day)
- Day 5: Orangerie + Rodin Museum, Catacombs, and a slow
  final evening on Ile Saint-Louis

9. PRACTICAL TIPS
-----------------------------------------------------
SAFETY
- Pickpocketing is the main risk: Metro line 1, around
  the Eiffel Tower, Louvre, Sacre-Coeur, and Gare du Nord.
- Use a zipped bag worn in front; don't keep your phone in
  a back pocket or on cafe tables.
- Ignore petition-signers, "found gold ring" tricks, and
  bracelet sellers.
- Emergency number: 112.

MONEY
- Use a forex card or a low-fee travel card; always choose
  to be charged in EUR (not INR) at card machines.
- Withdraw from bank ATMs, not standalone ones.

CONNECTIVITY
- Buy an eSIM before you fly, or a local SIM (Orange,
  SFR, Bouygues, Free). Many cafes and parks have free Wi-Fi.

TIMING
- Most museums close one day a week: Louvre on Tuesday,
  Orsay and Versailles on Monday. Plan around these.
- Sunday: many shops close, but the Marais stays lively.
- Arrive at major sights at opening time.

PACKING
- Comfortable walking shoes (cobblestones!)
- A light layer and compact umbrella, any season
- Universal adapter, power bank
- Smart-casual clothes for nicer restaurants
